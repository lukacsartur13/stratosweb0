Direction D — controlled Swiss editorial

One family, one weight, one width. Archivo at 400 and wdth 100% carries every word in every frame; there is no Medium and no second axis anywhere in the direction. Three type roles and no fourth: monument, editorial, micro. A 120 px frame on all four sides, a 1 200 px field divided into twelve columns, and two axes — the spine at column 1 and the counter-axis at column 9. No monument size is written anywhere: each is solved from the length of its own sentence, and its vertical position is whatever puts its last baseline on the scene's foot line. Monochrome, sentence case, no accent colour, no rules, no coordinates, no glow.

A · C · D — does D keep C's courage and gain A's stability?

Directions across, scenes down. Hungarian · English · German — equivalent authority, different geometry

Not one of these nine monuments shares a font-size with another, and that is the system working rather than failing. Hungarian is set by column fill; every other locale is solved to the same ink area — the product of total line advance and size — so that a language which needs more width gets less height and one that needs less width gets more. German's hero is the shortest statement in the study and sets largest at 170 px; German's high-altitude statement is the longest and sets at 144 px on three lines. The foot line does not move, because monuments are placed by their last baseline rather than by their top edge.

Four line breaks that are decisions, not defaults

Every break in the nine frames was set several ways and chosen. Most of the alternatives are rejected by a number that needs no picture — a one-line monument that solves to 99 px is not a judgement call. These four are: each is a break a careful person would have chosen, three of them are the break the content layer already carries, and each is rejected for a reason that only becomes visible in the frame. Chosen on the left, rejected on the right, solved by the same two rules and dropped into the same composition.

D1 · English — the frame overrules the sentence

Altitude is / what we build. 137 px, longest line 801 px, 178 px between the statement and the dial. · Altitude / is what we build. — the split messages.ts ships — solves to the same 137 px and the same presence, but its second line runs to 918 px and the air around the instrument collapses from 178 px to 81 px. Identical typography, and only one of them is D1.

D3 · Hungarian — the rag, and what a long second line costs

Innen már látni / a görbületet. 174 px, fill 88%, stopping 143 px short of the right margin. Line two is shorter, the rag falls away, the statement settles. · Innen már / látni a görbületet. 166 px, column-bound by its long second line at 97.7% fill, and 91% of the presence. It is both smaller and more cramped, and it buys a rhetorical pause the sentence does not need.

D3 · English — where presence does not decide, and the right edge does

From here / you can see / the curvature. 151 px on three lines, fill 71.5%, presence 100.2%. · From here you can / see the curvature. — the split messages.ts ships — 150 px on two lines, presence 100.1%. The ink areas are the same to within a tenth of a per cent, so this is not a presence decision. Its second line runs to 96.9% of the field, and that is the cramped right edge D3 rejected in Hungarian when the scene's fill came down from 0.96 to 0.88. Accepting it here and rejecting it there would make that principle a preference.

D3 · German — the frame the whole locale rule exists for

Von hier aus ist / die Krümmung / zu sehen. 144 px on three lines, 100% of the Hungarian frame's presence, 74.5% fill. · Von hier aus ist die / Krümmung zu sehen. German's best two-line break, column-bound at 132 px, holding 85%, and running to 98.3% of the field — the cramped right edge D3 rejects in Hungarian too. Keeping German on two lines to match Hungarian's silhouette is what a rigid template would do, and it costs the frame 15% of its authority and its right-hand air. The third line is not a concession; it is the locale's own geometry.
