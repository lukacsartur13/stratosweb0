The six acts — monochrome

Built and judged in monochrome before a single colour value was applied, because a composition that needs colour to be interesting is a composition that has not been solved. Direction D's system throughout: one family, one weight, one width axis at 100%, three type roles and no fourth, a 120 px margin on all four sides, a 1 200 px field in twelve columns, monuments anchored by their last baseline and sized from the length of their own sentence. No monument size is written anywhere in the study. Squint at this sheet before reading it: six large calm shapes, six different centres of gravity, and no two that reduce to the same silhouette.

The second beat of Act VI is not one of the six. §31 forbids fusing the arrival and the offer into one block, so the offer is its own frame: one direct question, one action, and nothing else in the picture.

The same six, in colour

Identical markup and identical solved typography — one CSS class is added and nothing else moves, so the two sheets cannot drift apart. Every blue is already in the repository: it is Sky.tsx's own atmosphere, from the ground haze to the 30 000 m ceiling. The background progresses by density rather than by altitude — flat near-black, gathering low, clearing, deepest blue, distance, ceiling — and not one state is announced at a chapter boundary. Yellow appears once, on the Rapidkert figure, after four consecutive frames without any.

The distance test — six acts at 240 px

§45's test, at the width of a thumbnail: at this scale each act must still have a silhouette of its own. What separates them here is not detail, because there is none left — it is where the mass sits. The Archivo width axis — three acts, three states

§48's proof sheet, and the only place in this study where the axis moves. Each row is one act's real composition with the fill, the foot line, the leading, the tracking and every other object held; the single variable is the axis. Note what the fill rule does with it: the fill is a promise about the longest LINE, so a narrower face reaches it at a larger size. 96% is not a smaller setting — it is a taller one at the same footprint, which is exactly the effect §9 hypothesises for the high-altitude frame. The measured gain is 3.4–3.7% of size and 3.1–3.7% of ink area, and the verdict is in §D of the report.

Hungarian · English · German — equivalent authority, different geometry

Eighteen monuments and no two of them share a font-size, which is the system working rather than failing. Hungarian is set by column fill; every other locale is solved to the same ink area — total line advance times size — so a language that needs more width gets less height and one that needs less width gets more. The foot line never moves, because monuments are placed by their last baseline. All eighteen sit within 0.8% of parity. Act IV is the exception that proves the rule: the figure is not translated, so all three locales solve to the same 179 px.

Two decisions, and the frames that made them

Both were built, lit, placed, measured and photographed before being cut, because §12 asks the composition to be authored first and checked afterwards — and a rejection is only evidence if the rejected thing exists.

1 · The second Altimeter appearance — §21, §34

The instrument here is not the hero's treatment at a smaller size — different pose, lower key, a 155 px dial against 229 px, its right edge on the right margin line and its centre on the monument's own last baseline. It is still the wrong object in the wrong frame: it terminates a composition that was already finished, and it draws the eye off a statement whose whole job is to be the first read. German decided it. `Sechs Bereiche, ein System.` sets the widest line in the study at 1 027 px and leaves 17 px between the end of the statement and the start of the dial — against 155 px in Hungarian. An object that has room in one language and none in another is not restrained, it is lucky. Act III ships with three objects, and the Altimeter appears twice in six acts.

2 · The three-line stack in Act II — §18, §22

Presence does not decide this one — 99.7% against 100%, which is inside the noise. The frame decides it. On two lines the act is a wide block on the right and it reads as the hero mirrored; on three it is a narrow column hung from the top of the frame, and the compression the act is about is carried by the setting instead of by anything added to the picture. It is also the only silhouette in the six that no other act comes near. The same comparison in German is six-act/y2.png, and the one-line arrival is six-act/y3.png — 71.7% of the presence, which needs no argument.
