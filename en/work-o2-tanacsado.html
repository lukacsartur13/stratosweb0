O2 Tanácsadó — leadership and HR consulting, Budapest

A website that speaks the language of business owners.

The two founders of O2 Tanácsadó have run their own company for 25 years, and they pass that practice on to other business owners. The new website carries their fresh positioning through word for word, and a self-check questionnaire gives a smaller first step to anyone for whom a personal conversation is still too big a one.

WebsitePositioned copySelf-check questionnaireEmail sequenceSEO

Sector leadership and HR consulting What we delivered website, questionnaire, email sequence and SEO Duration six weeks, September–October 2026 Live address o2tanacsado.hu

01Client context

Twenty-five years of running a company behind a generic HR site.

Tibor Marczali and Ádám Wortmann have run their own IT company for 25 years. At O2 Tanácsadó they pass that practice on to other business owners — leaders of companies with 20 to 200 people — through a company diagnosis, a monthly engagement, and leadership and team coaching.

The old website did not show any of this. It was a WordPress site of a few pages titled “Vállalati HR stratégia tervezés és kialakítás” (corporate HR strategy planning and design), offering general HR services.

In September we built the first new version on the existing, service-led message: service and regional pages, a blog. In October the client completed a new positioning strategy with a single promise: the company should run without the owner’s daily presence.

Copy without rewriting
Show the positioned copy word for word, following the copywriter’s notes.
Give first, then ask
Collect leads in a way that gives the visitor something useful for free first.
In the language of search, too
Keep HR consulting and organisational development as the site’s topics in search, while the copy talks about leadership situations.
One identity
A consistent, uncluttered look with the turquoise brand colour and better photos.

02Copy

The copy is the client’s. Word for word.

We received four copy documents: the text of the core pages, the questionnaire with its opt-in page, the welcome email sequence and an update. From them eight core pages were rebuilt: Home, Services, Monthly engagement, Company diagnosis, Leadership coaching, Team coaching, About and Contact.

We did not rewrite the copy, and we kept its bold emphasis. On the home page the three most important statements moved to the front, with the longer introduction in smaller type beneath them.

One template, four services
All four service pages follow the same order: the situation, what it includes, how it works, what to expect, frequently asked questions, a first conversation. If a page has no FAQ, for example, the template leaves that part out.
Where something was missing
Where the document flagged missing material, such as a client testimonial, the section was left out, or the document’s anonymous example took its place.
Rules the site keeps
An informal, personal voice. No invented testimonials, no client names or logos, and a single public price.
Checked by machine
Before every handover, every content line of the copy documents was compared by machine with the published pages, so a lost or altered sentence showed up at once.
The old addresses still work
We kept the old URLs, so existing links do not break.
Five fields
The contact form was cut down to five fields: name, company, email address, phone number and one question — “Röviden: mi az, ami most a legtöbb energiát viszi el a cégnél?” (In short: what is taking up the most energy at the company right now?)

03The first step

Fifteen minutes before you sit down.

For a business owner, sitting down with a consultant is a big step. So there is a smaller first step too: the questionnaire “Mennyire függ a cégem a személyes jelenlétemtől?” (How much does my company depend on my personal presence?), on its own page. On the home page the questionnaire and the free personal conversation stand side by side.

Sign-up

The visitor gives a first name and an email address and consents to the data processing.

Twelve statements

For each one they mark how true it is of their company, on a 0–3 scale.

Instant evaluation

They see their score straight away (0–36), the one of three bands that fits it, and a summary of their answers.

First email

The first email of the sequence arrives with the evaluation and a link to the results page.

Four more emails

On days 2, 5, 8 and 12 another email follows, with Tibor’s and Ádám’s stories and answers to the most common questions.

Personal conversation

Every step leads towards the free personal conversation.

Static site, small cloud function
The site stayed on static hosting. A Supabase Edge Function receives each submission: it saves it to the database so no lead is lost, notifies O2, and starts the five-email sequence in Resend.
Emails from the copy document
All five emails were built word for word from the copy document, each with an unsubscribe link.
A results link without a name
The link to the results page carries only the answers — no name, no email address.
Privacy built into the system
Submissions are deleted automatically after 12 months, the IP hash kept for abuse filtering after 30 days, and the contact at the email provider 30 days after the sequence ends. The privacy notice and the consent wording were updated to match.

04Search

The tone stays, the keyword gets in.

The copy talks about leadership situations (“Let the company run without your daily presence”), while business owners search for terms such as HR consulting or organisational development. We connected the two without touching the copywriter’s sentences.

Keyword page titles
Around 60 characters, for example “HR-tanácsadás kkv-knak havi együttműködésben” (HR consulting for SMEs in a monthly engagement).
Descriptions from the page’s own sentences
Meta descriptions under 160 characters, built only from that page’s own sentences.
The keyword in the main heading
The small label above the headline is part of the H1, so the keyword is in the main heading too, while the visible headline text did not change.
Descriptive internal links
On the home page service cards, the name of each service became a keyword internal link to its page.
Structured data
Schema.org markup for the company, the services, the frequently asked questions and the breadcrumbs.
Clean indexing
A sitemap, a self-referencing canonical on every page, and the preview environment kept out of search engines.

05Identity

One colour, two typefaces, one way with photos.

The brand colour became the turquoise of the logo; the earlier cobalt and royal blue accents are gone. Bricolage Grotesque for headings, Manrope for everything else; the earlier all-caps, letter-spaced labels were set in normal case too, so only these two typefaces really show.

The half-circle is drawn in code
The photos are freely licensed images chosen for each section’s topic. Each is black-and-white, circular, with a turquoise half-circle running across it. The half-circle is drawn in code rather than carried by the image file, so a new photo fits without any editing.
Matching portraits
One founder’s portrait was made from a photo taken outdoors: cropped at the shoulders, converted to black-and-white and set on the same grey circle as his partner’s.

06Details

Little maintenance, a lot of checking.

The site is built with Next.js; every page is pre-generated static HTML with no plugins behind it, so there is little to maintain. The preview updates itself after every change.

A button where it belongs
On a phone the “Beszéljünk” (Let’s talk) button sits at the bottom in a fixed bar. It does not appear on the questionnaire page, so it does not draw attention away from filling it in.
Build and static export
Before every handover: type checks, linting and a production build, plus a separate test of the static export.
On desktop and phone
Desktop and mobile views checked, with no horizontal overflow.
The questionnaire, clicked through
A full run-through before every handover, including the missing-answer warning and the results page.
Accessibility
Forms usable by keyboard, focus management and alternative text for images.

07Working together

A new positioning, on the site the next day.

Six weeks passed from the first version to the final identity refinements. The client’s new positioning strategy was finished on 13 October, and the next day the new copy was on the site.

8 September — first version

Service and regional pages, a blog with eight articles, a downloadable job description template, a quote request form, and a preview that updates itself.

7 October — turquoise

A turquoise brand colour in the logo, the favicon and the accents.

14 October — new copy

One day after the new positioning strategy: new copy on eight core pages, the self-check questionnaire, the welcome email sequence.

15 October — search

Keyword titles and descriptions, the keyword in the main heading.

20 October — identity

Two typefaces, a larger logo, new photos with the turquoise half-circle, a new portrait.

22 October — launch

The site on the o2tanacsado.hu domain, open to search engines, in place of the old WordPress site.

23 October — the questionnaire’s back end

A Supabase function and database, five email templates in Resend, automatic data deletion, an updated privacy notice.

08Lessons

What we take into the next one.

Five things that worked on this project and would work beyond a consultancy.

Positioning and SEO get along
The visible copy can stay the copywriter’s if the keywords go into the titles, the descriptions and the labels.
Lead generation starts with a small step
For a business owner, a 15-minute questionnaire is an easier first step than a personal conversation of an hour to an hour and a half.
A good template speeds up change
The day after the new positioning was finished, the new copy was already on the site.
Check word-for-word copy by machine
That way a lost or altered sentence shows up at once.
Static hosting can collect leads too
A small cloud function and an email service are enough, without moving hosts.

O2’s site now says what the founders say: the company should run without the owner’s daily presence. The copy is theirs, word for word; search engines meanwhile find HR consulting and organisational development; and anyone not yet ready to sit down with a consultant can start with a fifteen-minute questionnaire.

The site went live on 22 October 2026. We measure traffic and conversion from launch — how many people complete the questionnaire, how many ask for a personal conversation, and which searches the site appears for on Google — and we will publish those figures once there is a measured, verifiable number behind them.
