Where orders get lost

Someone scrolls through your products one evening, picks two and puts them in the basket. At checkout the delivery fee appears, then they are asked to register, then an error message comes up. They close the tab. All you see of this is a weaker month.

Half of building an online shop is development; the other half is deciding what the customer sees and when. We help with that half too, because that is where the order comes from.

The system

What goes into
building an
online store?

Products

Catalogue and product data

Variants, sizes, colours, bundles. We build the categories the way customers search, not the way the warehouse stores things. The two are rarely the same.

Money

Payment and delivery

Card payment, cash on delivery, bank transfer, couriers and parcel points. The delivery cost already shows in the basket, because the last step before payment is where most orders are lost.

Stock

Invoicing and stock integration

If you have invoicing or inventory software, we connect it so that stock, prices and orders are created in one place and nobody has to type anything in twice.

Measurement

Measurement from day one

From day one we measure add-to-basket, started checkouts, abandoned baskets and purchases. So even after the first month you know where you are losing customers.

Traffic

SEO and Shopping ads

We hand over category and product pages ready for search. If you want, we also run your Google Shopping and Performance Max ads, so the shop and its traffic are in one pair of hands.

The platform

the platform

We don't commit to any one system in advance. After the assessment we decide between a proven shop engine and custom development, based on the number of products, the invoicing and stock systems it has to connect to, and your plans for growth. The wrong platform costs more later than a good decision at the start.

If the shop already works and only visibility is missing, the answer is SEO: there the work is putting the category and product pages in order.

FAQ

Frequently asked
questions

Can't find your answer? Write to us — we're glad to help.

What does an online store cost?

It depends on how many products you have, in how many variants, and what the shop has to connect to. A shop with a few hundred products and one with several thousand are an order of magnitude apart, but both are priced with a one-off, itemised project fee. After the free discovery call you get an itemised proposal.

How long does it take?

We set the deadline in the proposal after the assessment, and we guarantee it, provided everything we need arrives on time. The longest stage is usually the product data: getting the photos, descriptions and variants in order.

Which platform will it be built on?

We decide after the assessment, depending on the number of products, the invoicing and stock systems it has to connect to and your plans for growth: it can be a proven shop engine or custom development. We don't commit in advance, and we explain the reasons for our recommendation.

Can you take over an existing store?

Yes. First we look at what can be saved: the product data and order history can usually be carried over. If the existing shop works and only visibility is missing, we tell you that too before we rebuild anything.

Do I get an admin interface?

Yes, cut down to the few things you will actually do: adding products, changing prices and stock, handling orders, coupons. Not a twenty-item panel of which you use three.

Who is responsible for legal compliance?

We build in the places for the mandatory content (terms and conditions, withdrawal information, privacy, cookie handling) and tell you what is missing. A lawyer is responsible for the content of those texts being correct; if you don't have one, we tell you in good time so that you do.

What if the store grows?

A new language, a new market or a new payment method can be added later, because we choose the platform with growth in mind from the start.
