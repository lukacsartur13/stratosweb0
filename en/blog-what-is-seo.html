What is SEO? A beginner's guide

SEO is a handful of dull decisions you either make or don't. There's no secret technique in it. This is about which ones you can make yourself and which are worth paying for.

Category SEO By the Stratos Webdesign team Published 25 August 2026 Updated 24 October 2026

SEO is the work that leads Google to judge that your page gives the best answer to a search. It has three layers: technology, content and time. Search Console, a Google Business Profile, good page titles and speed improvements can all be done for free, and the effect usually starts to show within 3–6 months.

SEO is the work that makes Google think your page answers best what somebody typed. It is neither a trick nor a deal: Google does not know what you sell, it only sees what you wrote, how the site is built, and how many people found it useful.

SEO has three layers: technology, content and time. The first can be fixed in an afternoon, the second takes months of writing, and the third cannot be bought.

What can you do without paying?

More than you think. The free part of SEO is the same work a specialist would do, only you do it. Four things that need no paid tool at all:

Google Search Console. Free, from Google, and it shows which terms you appear for, how many click, and which pages it could not crawl. Anyone discussing SEO without opening this is guessing.

Google Business Profile. If you have premises or a service area, for a local business this is often what brings the first enquiries. It is free, and we have written up the setup in eight steps.

Sorting out titles and headings. Every page needs its own <title> element naming the question that page answers. It is word-processing-level work and the most commonly skipped step.

Speed. PageSpeed Insights tells you free of charge what is slowing the site down. Some of the fixes (oversized images, unused fonts) need no developer.

When is it worth handing it to a specialist?

For SEO in Hungary you will see roughly three kinds of arrangement, and they differ mostly in scope.

A one-off audit typically costs somewhere between a few tens of thousands and a few hundred thousand forints (a typical market range, not our price). You get a list of what is wrong and work from there yourself. Good if you have a developer or marketer of your own to do it.

Ongoing work runs on monthly retainers. For a local service business this is considerably less than for a national online store, because there are fewer pages to fix and a smaller field to compete in.

Large one-off projects (a migration, adding languages, a rebuild) are priced per project. The spread is widest here, because most of the work is untangling the old system.

What you should not hear in any band: a guaranteed first position. First place is decided by Google's ranking, which changes constantly. Anyone guaranteeing it either does not understand, or does and says it anyway.

What our monthly SEO service includes (a monthly report, the technical basics, Google Business Profile, visibility in AI search) is described on our SEO page. You get the price in an individual proposal after the free discovery call.

How long before it shows?

Technical fixes can show within weeks, because Google re-crawls the site. Movement from content usually starts to show within 3–6 months. That's how the system works: Google first watches what people do with your page, and only then decides.

That's why switching provider every few months is a bad idea: the results of the work often start to show just when you'd give up.

SEO in the age of AI search

The answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini are built on websites too. If you answer your buyers' questions clearly, with accurate facts and on well-structured pages, you have a better chance of appearing there as well.

What not to do

Do not buy links. Do not copy your competitor's copy. Do not stuff the same phrase everywhere: Google has understood synonyms for more than a decade, and keyword stuffing now does more harm than good. And do not start at all if the site is so slow or confusing that the visitor bounces anyway: fix the site first.

SEO is about turning up for the right question. A hundred visitors looking for what you sell are worth more than ten thousand who are not.
