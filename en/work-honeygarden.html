HoneyGarden garden design — Győr, Budapest, Lake Balaton

Before the garden, there is a plan.

A garden designer hands over a plan, not a garden. So Hanna Dóbiás’s website is built like a plan sheet: a site plan, a scale, layers — and exactly what the client ends up holding.

WebsiteCustom drawing languageLocal SEOBilingualAI visibility

Sector garden design, landscape architecture What we delivered website and local SEO Languages Hungarian and English Live address honeygarden.hu

01Client context

A garden designer’s product does not fit in a photo.

Hanna Dóbiás is a landscape and garden engineer who designs private gardens in Győr, Budapest and around Lake Balaton under the name HoneyGarden. Four plan types are on offer: a concept plan, a planting plan, construction drawings and a 3D visualisation.

Most garden websites show finished gardens. For a designer, that leaves the more important question open: what does the client get for the money before anyone starts digging? Most visitors do not know the difference between a concept plan and construction drawings — or why they should pay for a plan when the contractor will build the garden anyway.

So the brief was not a pretty garden website, but one that shows the product itself: the plan.

02Design direction

The garden is a drawing first. So is the site.

The whole site is made from the materials of a plan sheet: paper-coloured ground, ink lines, and living colour only where there are plants. Across the top of the opening screen runs the label strip of a plan sheet — drawing number, scale, coordinates — and below it the site plan of a family garden, with Hanna’s name in the title block.

The type comes from the drawing board too: a monospace for plan labels, a wide grotesque for headings, an italic serif for personal emphasis. On the very first screen, visitors see what they will hold at the end of the commission.

Every drawing is made in code
Site plan, bubble diagram, planting symbols, section, isometric garden model, flowering calendar, regional maps — all vector drawings in one shared drawing language. There is no stock illustration on the site.
First person singular
Hanna designs alone, so the site speaks in the first person and on first-name terms: “Hi, I’m Hanna.” The About page is built on Hanna’s own text.
Layers instead of cards
The four plan types build on one another, so the site does not show them as four identical cards but as four layers on the same sheet.
A process instead of a price list
The design fee depends on the plot and the plans requested, so there is no price list. Instead, the site describes what happens after the first message: a reply within two working days, a site visit, a written, itemised quote.

03One plot, four layers

Four services, one sheet.

In the middle of the home page sits a dark section with the site plan of a plot. As you scroll, layer after layer is added: the zones of the concept, the planting, the construction details — and finally the four layers open out in space, the way a 3D visualisation shows them together.

The list of services is the process.

Visitors do not compare four boxes. They watch an empty plot become a garden, and on the way they learn what each plan adds. Every layer leads on to its own service page.

On a phone, the site plan stays at the top of the screen while the steps scroll beneath it, and moves with the header as it hides on scroll.

04Scope delivered

What the finished site contains

Everything below can be checked on the published site.

Four service pages, four drawings
A bubble diagram for the concept plan, planting symbols for the planting plan, a build-up section for the construction plan, an isometric garden model for the visualisation. Every page answers the same four questions: what you get, who it is for, how it is made, and what people usually ask next.
A live flowering calendar
When twelve plants flower or look their best, across the whole year. The vertical line always sits on the current month, so visitors see what is in season right now.
A 3D model to try
The garden model starts as a line drawing and colours itself into a visualisation within a few seconds. Both the view (line drawing, visualisation) and the season (summer, autumn, winter) can be switched.
Mood board
On the concept plan page, materials and colours on arched swatches: limestone, larch, Corten, sage, lavender, gravel. In Hanna’s words: “Before there’s a line, there’s a mood.”
A measuring crosshair on the plan
When the pointer moves over the site plan on the home page, the sheet turns slightly towards it, and a crosshair shows in metres where the cursor is on the plot.
Motion that draws
Drawings and line icons draw themselves as they come into view, and on hover, plan-sheet “+” marks appear at the corners of framed elements. With reduced motion switched on, the movement is left out.
About and enquiry
Hanna’s photo in a drawn arch, with degree and contact details beside it. On the enquiry form, the exact address can be given so the plot can be checked on satellite imagery before the site visit — but the town is enough.
Two languages
Hungarian and English versions, paired page by page: twenty pages in all.

05Local search

Three regions, three separate pages.

Hanna does site visits in three regions, so each one has its own page. Not the same text with the town name swapped: each is about the region’s own questions.

Győr and Győr-Moson-Sopron
Wind and open landscape, groundwater and drainage, plants suited to the soils of the Little Hungarian Plain, privacy on the smaller plots of new housing areas.
Budapest and its surroundings
Sloping plots, small footprints, screening from the neighbours, and the urban heat island: shade trees, permeable paving.
Lake Balaton
Stony or sandy soil, seasonal use, water-saving irrigation, the view of the lake and a shaded terrace.
On all three pages
A schematic regional map, the list of towns in the design area, local FAQs, and links to the other two regions.
In the search engines’ language
Structured data on every page (the business, its services with the area served, breadcrumbs, FAQs), Hungarian–English language pairs, a sitemap, short URLs such as /kerttervezes-gyor, and Google Search Console verification.
Readable for AI assistants too
An llms.txt summary and an llms-full.txt with the full text of the site. The robots.txt allows the AI crawlers of OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and Google by name.

06Working together

Three rounds, nine days.

Hanna’s feedback came in short lists, as the work went on. The biggest change came in the second round: the business changed its name, from Dóbiás Hanna kerttervezés to HoneyGarden.

The site’s text and contact details do not live in the finished HTML but in the source files every page is generated from. So the new name reached every page, the data for search engines and the AI summary in a single step.

13 October — first version

Twenty pages in two languages: home page, four service pages, three region pages, About and enquiry.

16 October — first round

A new section order on the home page, a new argument for climate-adaptive plants, a fifth step in the process with aftercare advice, and an address field for a satellite pre-survey.

20 October — second round

A new name and logo: HoneyGarden. A new line on the opening image, Hanna’s photo in the drawn arch, and Hanna’s own text on the About page.

21 October — launch

The site goes live on the honeygarden.hu domain, over HTTPS.

22 October — third round

Short URLs without .html, the final logo, layout refinements on desktop and phone.

07Details

What nobody notices when it works.

The phone view is not a shrunken desktop: long Hungarian words, large headings and a crowded header each needed their own solution.

Hungarian words, in capitals
“Növénykiültetési” (planting) does not fit on one line as a large all-caps heading. The browser’s own hyphenation is switched off, and the break was placed by hand at the right syllable: NÖVÉNY-KIÜLTETÉSI.
A header that fits the narrowest phone
The logo, the language switch and the menu button fit on one line on small screens; below 480 pixels the “Menu” label is there for screen readers only.
Windows laptops with display scaling
At 150–200% display scaling the browser sees the laptop as a narrow screen, and the navigation used to disappear. It now stays visible down to 860 pixels; below that the menu button takes over.
One source, no external libraries
A single Node script generates the pages from the source files, with no framework. All of the site’s behaviour lives in one JavaScript file of about 10 KB; hosting is static, on GitHub Pages.
