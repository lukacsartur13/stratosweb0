Use of cookies

The website uses cookies to improve the user experience, produce statistical analysis and for marketing purposes. Cookies may be session, analytical, functional or marketing cookies. Users may consent to the use of cookies and can delete or block them in their browser at any time.

Types of cookie

  • Session cookies — stay on your device only until you leave the website.
  • Persistent cookies — depending on browser settings, stay on your device for longer, or until you delete them.
  • Third-party cookies — placed in your browser by a third party (e.g. Google Analytics).

Strictly necessary session cookies

These are essential for navigating the site and for its functions to work. Without accepting them the site, or parts of it, may not display at all or may display incorrectly.

Analytical or performance cookies

These help distinguish visitors and collect data on how visitors behave on the site. They do not collect information capable of identifying you; data is stored in aggregated, anonymous form (e.g. Google Analytics).

Functional cookies

These cookies exist to improve the user experience. They detect and store, for example, which device you opened the site with, or data you previously entered and asked to be saved: automatic sign-in, chosen language, text size, typeface or other customisable elements. These cookies do not track your activity on other websites.

Targeting or advertising cookies

These let websites present the information most relevant to your interests. They require your explicit consent. These cookies collect detailed information about your browsing habits.

Do cookies contain personal data?

Most cookies contain no personal information and cannot be used to identify users. The stored data serves more convenient browsing and is stored so that unauthorised persons cannot access it.

Security considerations

Cookies are not viruses or spyware. Being plain text files they cannot be executed and are therefore not programs. It is possible, however, for information to be hidden in them maliciously, in which case they can act as spyware. For that reason antivirus programs may continually flag cookies for deletion.

Because the device you browse with and the web servers communicate constantly, an attacker who intervenes in that process can extract the information stored in cookies. One cause of this can be, for example, an inadequately encrypted Wi-Fi configuration.

Managing and deleting cookies

Cookies can be deleted or blocked in the browser you use. Browsers allow cookies to be set by default; this can be disabled in the browser settings and existing cookies deleted. You can also set the browser to notify you when a cookie is sent to your device. Note that blocking or restricting these files degrades the browsing experience and may cause faults in the website's functionality. The settings are usually found in the browser's “Options” or “Settings” menu.
